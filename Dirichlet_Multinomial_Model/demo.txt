The path of a lightning channel is often long, branched, and tortuous. This complex geometry causes sound waves from different parts of the channel to reach an observer at different times. As a result, thunder is not just a single sound but a series of claps, rolls, and rumbles. The duration and character of these sounds depend on the distance and the specific path the lightning takes. The farther away the lightning is, the longer and more rumbling the thunder will sound. Related Topics: lightning thunder. The time delay between seeing lightning and hearing thunder can be used to estimate the distance of the lightning strike from an observer’s location. Every three seconds of delay between the flash and the thunder correspond to one kilometer, and every five seconds correspond to one mile.
